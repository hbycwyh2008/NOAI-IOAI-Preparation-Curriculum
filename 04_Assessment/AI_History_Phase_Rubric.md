# AI History and Thinking Humans — Phase Rubric

Use this public rubric for formative feedback and transparent phase-gate decisions. Do not place protected answer keys, calibration examples, or scored benchmark responses in the public repository.

## Scoring Scale

- **4 — Independent mastery:** accurate, evidence-based, transferable, and independently reconstructed.
- **3 — Secure:** accurate on familiar cases with minor prompting or limited transfer.
- **2 — Developing:** partial understanding, weak evidence boundaries, or excessive dependence on examples.
- **1 — Beginning:** recalls terms but cannot use them to analyse a claim.
- **0 — No evidence:** missing, copied, or unrelated work.

## Criteria

| Criterion | 4 — Independent mastery | 3 — Secure | 2 — Developing | 1 — Beginning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Historical map** | accurately connects symbolic AI, neural networks, machine learning, deep learning, reinforcement learning, AI springs, and AI winters through causes and limitations | accurate sequence with most causal links | several correct events but weak relationships | isolated names or dates without structure |
| **Task formalisation** | independently identifies input, output, training signal, metric, and deployment boundary for unfamiliar systems | correctly formalises familiar systems | confuses one element or needs prompting | relies on model names or keywords without formalising the task |
| **Claim and evidence** | clearly separates author claim, evidence, supported conclusion, and unsupported extension | uses relevant evidence with mostly clear boundaries | offers examples but overstates what they prove | gives opinion or summary without evidence |
| **Capability–limitation analysis** | identifies characteristic capability, data/objective/evaluation constraints, and realistic failure modes | identifies major strengths and limitations | lists limitations without mechanism | treats success or failure as universal |
| **Concept distinctions** | accurately distinguishes recognition, prediction, generation, reasoning, abstraction, analogy, understanding, and consciousness | distinguishes most concepts with appropriate examples | examples overlap or definitions remain vague | uses the terms interchangeably |
| **Counterargument** | presents a serious opposing view, responds fairly, and states what evidence could change the conclusion | includes a relevant counterargument and response | mentions an opposing view without genuine engagement | dismisses alternatives or omits them |
| **Independent reconstruction** | produces an original, coherent artifact and explains it without copying notes | produces a complete artifact with minor dependence on scaffolds | artifact is incomplete or closely follows the teacher model | copied, missing, or cannot be explained |
| **Communication and uncertainty** | uses precise language, marks uncertainty, and avoids anthropomorphic overclaiming | generally precise with minor overstatement | uneven precision or unsupported certainty | rhetorical certainty replaces analysis |

## Phase-Gate Decision

A student is **Ready for Andrew Ng Machine Learning** when:

- the total score is at least **24/32**;
- no criterion is below **2**;
- Task Formalisation, Claim and Evidence, and Capability–Limitation Analysis are each at least **3**;
- the student independently applies the AI Claim Audit to a new system.

## Reteaching Decision

Use targeted reteaching rather than repeating the whole phase:

- low Historical Map → rebuild the two-track technical/expectation timeline;
- low Task Formalisation → practise `X`, `y`, reward, prediction, and metric identification;
- low Claim and Evidence → use claim–evidence–boundary colour coding;
- low Capability–Limitation Analysis → test distribution shift, shortcut learning, and reward loopholes;
- low Concept Distinctions → use contrasting examples rather than additional definitions;
- low Independent Reconstruction → remove scaffolds gradually and require oral explanation.

## Required Final Evidence

The **Thinking Human’s AI Brief** must contain:

1. an AI-history map;
2. a capability–limitation matrix;
3. a defensible position on the intelligence of current AI;
4. at least three cases from the book;
5. one serious counterargument;
6. a statement of what new evidence could change the conclusion;
7. a complete AI-assistance record when applicable.
