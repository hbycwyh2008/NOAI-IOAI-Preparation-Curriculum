# Session 33 — What Counts as Intelligence?

**Class duration:** 70 minutes  
**Required reading before class:** Prologue and Chapter 1  
**Essential question:** What evidence is required before a machine should be described as intelligent?

## Required Mastery

Students must be able to:

1. Explain that artificial intelligence is a research field rather than one single technique.
2. Describe the central ambitions of early artificial-intelligence research.
3. Explain the symbolic-AI intuition: represent knowledge with symbols and manipulate those symbols with rules or search.
4. Explain what the Turing test attempts to measure and what it cannot prove.
5. Distinguish intelligent behaviour, human-like behaviour, understanding, and consciousness.
6. Identify when the term **AI** is being used as a field, a system, a capability, or a marketing label.
7. Separate an observable system behaviour from a larger unsupported claim about the system’s internal state.
8. Identify the evidence that would be needed to strengthen or weaken a claim about machine intelligence.

## Misconceptions to Reject

- Conversational fluency proves human-like understanding.
- Artificial intelligence began only with modern deep learning.
- AI and machine learning are exact synonyms.
- Passing one behavioural test proves consciousness.

## Core Pattern

```text
AI claim
→ observable behaviour
→ test conditions
→ evidence
→ supported conclusion
→ unsupported conclusion
```

## 70-Minute Learning Cycle

| Time | Block | Required student action |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–7 | **Skill Warm-Up** | Sort statements into field, system, capability, or marketing use of the term AI. |
| 7–14 | **Talk Robin 1** | Compare one central claim, one supporting example, and one unresolved question from the reading. |
| 14–20 | **Entry Check** | Explain the purpose and limits of the Turing test without reopening the book. |
| 20–32 | **Core Pattern** | Learn how to separate observed behaviour from claims about understanding or consciousness. |
| 32–48 | **Guided Practice** | Audit the claim: “A system can sustain a convincing conversation, therefore it understands language in the same way a person does.” |
| 48–62 | **Independent Rebuild** | Create a concept map connecting problem solving, learning, perception, language, reasoning, understanding, and consciousness. Mark which connections are supported and which remain open questions. |
| 62–70 | **Talk Robin 2 + Evidence** | Explain the concept map and submit the AI-claim audit. |

## Reading Evidence Required Before Class

- one-sentence statement of the author’s central claim;
- one case used as evidence;
- one unresolved question;
- one AI claim that should be tested rather than accepted immediately.

A plot summary alone does not count. Students must distinguish the author’s claim, the evidence in the text, and their own judgement.

## Exit Evidence

Explain in two minutes why completing an intelligent task does not by itself prove human-like intelligence.

## Gate

The student passes only when the explanation identifies both the demonstrated behaviour and the larger conclusion that has not yet been established.
