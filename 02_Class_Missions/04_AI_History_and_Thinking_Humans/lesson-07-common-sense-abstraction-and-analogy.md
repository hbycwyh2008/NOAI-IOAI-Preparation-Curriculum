# Session 39 — Common Sense, Abstraction, and Analogy

**Class duration:** 70 minutes  
**Required reading before class:** Chapters 14–15  
**Essential question:** What additional abilities are involved in human-like understanding?

## Required Mastery

Students must be able to:

1. Explain why understanding cannot be captured by one simple metric.
2. Distinguish memorisation, interpolation, generalisation, transfer, and abstraction.
3. Explain the grounding problem: how symbols or words connect to experience and the world.
4. Identify physical, social, intentional, causal, and situational common sense.
5. Explain abstraction as preserving transferable structure while ignoring irrelevant surface detail.
6. Explain analogy as mapping relations between different domains rather than matching appearances.
7. Identify where an analogy works and where it breaks.
8. Explain why success on a nearby test distribution is not unlimited transfer.
9. Recognise the roles of prior knowledge, small-sample learning, and embodied experience in human learning.
10. Provide separate examples of pattern recognition, generalisation, abstraction, analogy, and understanding.

## Misconceptions to Reject

- Because understanding has no perfect definition, it cannot be studied.
- Memorising many examples necessarily creates an abstract concept.
- Analogy is only surface similarity.
- Success on one new test set proves transfer to every new domain.

## Core Pattern

```text
surface features
→ relational structure
→ abstraction
→ transfer
→ new situation
```

## 70-Minute Learning Cycle

| Time | Block | Required student action |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–7 | **Skill Warm-Up** | Distinguish memorisation, interpolation, transfer, abstraction, and analogy in short examples. |
| 7–14 | **Talk Robin 1** | Compare one claim about understanding with one example that challenges it. |
| 14–20 | **Entry Check** | Explain why analogy is based on relations rather than appearance alone. |
| 20–32 | **Core Pattern** | Extract relational structure and transfer it beyond surface details. |
| 32–48 | **Guided Practice** | Compare a surface-association problem with a relational analogy and identify the background knowledge required by each. |
| 48–62 | **Independent Rebuild** | Choose an analogy and identify its source domain, target domain, relation mapping, transferable structure, and failure boundary. |
| 62–70 | **Talk Robin 2 + Evidence** | Explain why the analogy is useful and where it becomes misleading. |

## Exit Evidence

Give distinct examples of pattern recognition, generalisation, abstraction, analogy, and understanding.

## Gate

The student passes only when the examples are genuinely different and the analogy includes both a valid structural mapping and a limitation.
