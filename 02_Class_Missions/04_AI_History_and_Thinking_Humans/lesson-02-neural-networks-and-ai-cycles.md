# Session 34 — Neural Networks, Machine Learning, and AI Cycles

**Class duration:** 70 minutes  
**Required reading before class:** Chapters 2–3  
**Essential question:** Why has artificial intelligence repeatedly moved through periods of optimism and disappointment?

## Required Mastery

Students must be able to:

1. Explain that artificial neural networks are inspired by biology but are not accurate replicas of brains.
2. Identify inputs, weights, weighted combinations, outputs, errors, and parameter updates.
3. Distinguish hand-written rules from parameters learned from data.
4. Formalise supervised learning with input `X`, label `y`, prediction, error, and update.
5. Explain why early neural-network approaches were limited.
6. Explain how data, computing power, and algorithmic improvements contributed to later success.
7. Describe AI spring and AI winter as interactions among technical results, promises, funding, and public expectations.
8. Distinguish symbolic AI, machine learning, neural networks, and deep learning.
9. Explain why progress on one benchmark does not justify every broader prediction made about AI.

## Misconceptions to Reject

- Neural networks think exactly like biological brains.
- More data guarantees that every problem can be solved.
- Deep learning has replaced every other AI method.
- An AI winter proves that all work in the field was useless.

## Core Pattern

```text
early success
→ broad promise
→ hidden limitation
→ real-world failure
→ reduced confidence
→ new technical conditions
```

## 70-Minute Learning Cycle

| Time | Block | Required student action |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–7 | **Skill Warm-Up** | Match key developments with symbolic AI, neural networks, machine learning, or deep learning. |
| 7–14 | **Talk Robin 1** | Compare one technical development with one change in public expectation. |
| 14–20 | **Entry Check** | Explain the difference between a programmed rule and a learned parameter. |
| 20–32 | **Core Pattern** | Trace how evidence, promises, investment, and limitations create AI cycles. |
| 32–48 | **Guided Practice** | Compare a medical decision system built from explicit expert rules with one trained from labelled patient records. |
| 48–62 | **Independent Rebuild** | Build a two-track timeline: technical developments on one track and public expectations, investment, or disappointment on the other. |
| 62–70 | **Talk Robin 2 + Evidence** | Defend one connection between a technical result and a change in expectations. |

## Exit Evidence

Explain both:

- how machine learning differs from a traditional rule-based program;
- why a major benchmark improvement does not prove that human-level general intelligence is close.

## Gate

The student must connect technical evidence to the historical cycle without treating either technological optimism or pessimism as proof by itself.
