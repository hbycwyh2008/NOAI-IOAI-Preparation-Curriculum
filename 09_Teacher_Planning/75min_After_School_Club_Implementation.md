# 75-Minute After-School Club Implementation

This curriculum is delivered as an after-school club. Ordinary lessons must follow the classroom-flow poster exactly.

## Core Message

**WE LEARN. PRACTICE. REBUILD. SHARE.**

The goal is not just to finish the task. The goal is to **rebuild and explain it independently**.

## Required 7-Step Flow

| Step | Block | Time | What happens |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | **Skill Warm-Up** | 0–8 min | Students use a video, guide, or task to meet the idea. The teacher does not give a long lecture here. |
| 2 | **Talk Robin 1** | 8–15 min | Pair discussion: what was done and what is confusing. Every student speaks. |
| 3 | **Entry Check** | 15–22 min | Students answer a short cold check so the teacher can see whether the basics are ready. |
| 4 | **Core Pattern** | 22–35 min | Teacher explains the key method or pattern clearly, using one clean example. |
| 5 | **Guided Practice** | 35–53 min | Students practise with teacher support. They ask questions, repair errors, and improve. |
| 6 | **Independent Rebuild** | 53–67 min | Students recreate the pattern on their own with less help. This block must not be removed. |
| 7 | **Talk Robin 2 + Evidence** | 67–75 min | Students summarize, explain, and submit proof of learning. |

## Teacher Operating Rules

1. Keep the seven blocks in the poster order.
2. Do not replace **Skill Warm-Up** with a long teacher lecture.
3. Do not skip **Talk Robin 1**; it reveals confusion before deeper teaching.
4. Do not skip **Entry Check**; it protects the lesson from moving forward blindly.
5. Keep **Core Pattern** short and precise: one method, one model, one pattern.
6. Preserve **Independent Rebuild** even when time is tight.
7. End with **Talk Robin 2 + Evidence**, not with uncollected practice.

## Evidence Standard

By the end of each ordinary club lesson, every student should have at least one piece of evidence:

- a completed worksheet section;
- a trace table, calculation, notebook cell, debugging log, or short written explanation;
- an independent rebuild or modified task;
- a short oral explanation from Talk Robin 2;
- a Git commit or equivalent submitted proof when coding work is involved.

## How to Handle Time Pressure

When time is short, reduce the size of the task, not the structure of the lesson.

| Problem | Do this | Do not do this |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up takes too long | Use a shorter clip, smaller guide, or one prepared task | Remove Talk Robin 1 |
| Students are confused | Shorten the guided task and fix one core misconception | Lecture for 30 minutes |
| Guided practice overruns | Reduce the number of examples | Remove Independent Rebuild |
| Evidence submission is slow | Accept a smaller proof item and finish submission after class | End without Talk Robin 2 + Evidence |

## Special Sessions

Long contest sessions are exceptions. Round 1 mocks, past-paper reproduction, Round 2 projects, and full simulations may use extended timing. They should still preserve the same instructional logic where possible:

1. warm up or rules check;
2. first discussion or task interpretation;
3. readiness check;
4. core strategy/pattern;
5. supported work;
6. independent performance;
7. evidence, explanation, and post-mortem.

Do not compress a 120-, 150-, 240-, or 360-minute simulation into one ordinary 75-minute club lesson.
