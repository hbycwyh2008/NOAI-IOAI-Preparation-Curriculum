# 75-Minute After-School Club Implementation

The canonical pathway is delivered as an after-school club. Ordinary lessons use the seven-step 75-minute structure. The Bohrium foundation sequence and AI History reading seminars are named 70-minute exceptions; long mocks and simulations use their stated competition-realistic duration.

## Core Message

**WE LEARN. PRACTICE. REBUILD. SHARE.**

The goal is not just to finish the task. The goal is to **rebuild and explain it independently**.

## Required 7-Step Flow

| Step | Block | Time | What happens |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | **Skill Warm-Up** | 0–8 min | Students retrieve or encounter the key idea through a short video segment, reading check, guide, or prepared task. The teacher does not give a long lecture here. |
| 2 | **Talk Robin 1** | 8–15 min | Pair discussion: what was understood, what evidence supports it, and what remains confusing. Every student speaks. |
| 3 | **Entry Check** | 15–22 min | Students answer a short cold check so the teacher can see whether the prerequisites are ready. |
| 4 | **Core Pattern** | 22–35 min | The teacher explains one key method, model, or reasoning pattern using one clean example. |
| 5 | **Guided Practice** | 35–53 min | Students practise with teacher support, repair errors, and improve. |
| 6 | **Independent Rebuild** | 53–67 min | Students reconstruct or transfer the pattern independently with less help. This block must not be removed. |
| 7 | **Talk Robin 2 + Evidence** | 67–75 min | Students summarise, explain, and submit proof of learning. |

## Teacher Operating Rules

1. Keep the seven blocks in the stated order.
2. Do not replace **Skill Warm-Up** with a long teacher lecture or first exposure to a full external course segment.
3. Do not skip **Talk Robin 1**; it reveals confusion before deeper teaching.
4. Do not skip **Entry Check**; it protects the lesson from moving forward blindly.
5. Keep **Core Pattern** short and precise: one method, one model, or one reasoning pattern.
6. Preserve **Independent Rebuild** even when time is tight.
7. End with **Talk Robin 2 + Evidence**, not with uncollected practice.
8. Assign long videos and Phase 04 book chapters before class when the lesson file requires them.

## Evidence Standard

By the end of each ordinary club lesson, every student should have at least one piece of evidence:

- a completed worksheet or reading-evidence section;
- a trace table, calculation, concept map, notebook cell, debugging log, or short written explanation;
- an independent rebuild or modified task;
- a short oral explanation from Talk Robin 2;
- a Git commit or equivalent submitted proof when coding work is involved;
- an AI-assistance record when assistance was used.

## How to Handle Time Pressure

When time is short, reduce the size of the task, not the structure of the lesson.

| Problem | Do this | Do not do this |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up takes too long | use a shorter clip, smaller retrieval task, or one prepared prompt | remove Talk Robin 1 |
| Reading was not completed | use the entry check to identify the gap and apply the phase policy | silently convert the seminar into seventy minutes of first reading |
| Students are confused | shorten the guided task and fix one core misconception | lecture for 30 minutes |
| Guided practice overruns | reduce the number of examples | remove Independent Rebuild |
| Evidence submission is slow | accept a smaller proof item and finish upload after class | end without Talk Robin 2 + Evidence |

## Named 70-Minute Exceptions

### Bohrium ML Foundations — Sessions 19–32

Use the exact named flow in the Bohrium lesson files. Verify authenticated access and assigned video structure before the cohort begins.

### AI History and Thinking Humans — Sessions 33–40

Use:

| Time | Block |
|---:|---|
| 0–7 | Skill Warm-Up |
| 7–14 | Talk Robin 1 |
| 14–20 | Entry Check |
| 20–32 | Core Pattern |
| 32–48 | Guided Practice |
| 48–62 | Independent Rebuild |
| 62–70 | Talk Robin 2 + Evidence |

The assigned book reading occurs before class. The classroom is for retrieval, evidence testing, discussion, reconstruction, and explanation.

## Extended Sessions

Long contest sessions are exceptions. Round 1 mocks, past-paper reproduction, Round 2 projects, and full simulations may use extended timing. They should still preserve the same instructional logic where possible:

1. warm-up or rules check;
2. task interpretation;
3. readiness check;
4. core strategy or pattern;
5. supported work;
6. independent performance;
7. evidence, explanation, and postmortem.

Do not compress a 120-, 150-, 240-, or 360-minute simulation into one ordinary 75-minute club lesson.
