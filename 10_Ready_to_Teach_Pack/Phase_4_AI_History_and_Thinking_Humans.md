# Phase 4 Teacher Pack — AI History and Thinking Humans

**Sessions:** 33–40  
**Duration:** eight 70-minute reading seminars  
**Core text:** Melanie Mitchell, *Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans*

## Purpose

This phase prepares students to study machine-learning models without confusing benchmark performance, fluent output, or commercial claims with general intelligence or human-like understanding.

## Required Teacher Preparation

Before the phase begins:

- confirm that every student has legal access to the book;
- publish the eight reading assignments and due dates;
- model the difference among an author claim, textual evidence, and a student judgement;
- prepare one current AI claim for each seminar’s guided audit;
- print or assign the [Reading Evidence Template](../03_Templates/AI_History_Reading_Evidence_Template.md);
- review the [Phase Rubric](../04_Assessment/AI_History_Phase_Rubric.md);
- decide how students will submit the final **Thinking Human’s AI Brief**.

| Session | Reading | Required conceptual outcome | Required independent evidence |

| 34 | Chapters 2–3 | explains symbolic AI, machine learning, neural networks, AI springs, and AI winters | two-track historical timeline |
| 35 | Chapters 4–5 | formalises image classification and bounds benchmark claims | visual-capability ladder |
| 36 | Chapters 6–7 | explains shortcut learning, distribution shift, robustness, and responsibility | AI trust checklist |

| 38 | Chapters 11–13 | distinguishes fluent language processing from grounded understanding | language-capability ladder |
| 39 | Chapters 14–15 | distinguishes memorisation, generalisation, abstraction, analogy, and understanding | analogy map with failure boundary |

## Standard 70-Minute Seminar

| Time | Block | Teacher responsibility |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–7 | Skill Warm-Up | retrieve concepts without allowing students to reopen the text |
| 7–14 | Talk Robin 1 | listen for unsupported claims and reading misunderstandings |
| 14–20 | Entry Check | verify that the reading was completed and understood |
| 20–32 | Core Pattern | teach one transferable claim-analysis structure |
| 32–48 | Guided Practice | analyse one case with visible evidence and claim boundaries |
| 48–62 | Independent Rebuild | require an original reconstruction, not copied notes |
| 62–70 | Talk Robin 2 + Evidence | check explanation quality and collect the artifact |

## Non-Negotiable Distinctions

Students must repeatedly distinguish:

- artificial intelligence as a field from one particular model;
- symbolic rules from learned parameters;
- labels from rewards;
- classification from understanding;
- fluency from factual reliability;
- prediction from causality;
- benchmark success from real-world robustness;
- narrow task performance from general intelligence;
- abstraction and analogy from surface similarity;
- uncertainty from unsupported certainty.

## Phase Gate

A student advances to Andrew Ng Machine Learning only when the student can independently:

1. reconstruct the major AI-history timeline;
2. compare symbolic AI, supervised learning, deep learning, and reinforcement learning;
3. formalise at least three tasks using input, output, training signal, and metric;
4. explain why benchmark success is a bounded claim;
5. identify data, objective, evaluation, and deployment limitations;
6. apply the ten-question AI Claim Audit to a new system;
7. defend a position using evidence, a counterargument, and a stated condition for changing the conclusion.

## Reteaching Triggers

Reteach before advancement when a student:

- summarises chapters but cannot identify the author’s evidence;
- treats a correct output as proof of understanding;
- uses terms such as *AI*, *learning*, or *intelligence* without defining the task;
- cannot separate `X`, `y`, reward, prediction, and evaluation;
- gives a strong opinion without a falsifiable claim or evidence boundary;
- copies the teacher’s concept map instead of rebuilding it.
